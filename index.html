Object-level Semantic Mapping with Language Models from Egocentric Video

Masters thesis

I did my masters thesis at the Oxford Robotics Institute in the Dynamic Robot Systems group under Prof. Maurice Fallon. The robotics platform I worked with was rather unconventional — Meta's Project Aria AR Research Glasses.

This work explored how hand-object interactions can be detected from egocentric video, as part of the group's wider research into change detection and scene understanding. I trained an LSTM on sliding windows of hand- and eye-tracking data to predict when the user is interacting with an object, and an open-vocabulary Vision-Language Model to identify the type of object. I combined this with the glasses' SLAM data to determine the 3D position of objects over time, and maintain a semantic memory of interactions for downstream querying with an LLM to answer questions such as "Where did I leave my keys?" or "Did I turn the oven off?"

Synthetic Training Data for Aerial Object Classification

Machine Vision Engineering Internship

During an internship at defence startup Quell I experimented with how synthetic data could be used to train aerial object detection and classification models. I used the Unity game engine and Unity Perception Package to generate and annotate images at scale, then performed ablation studies comparing model performance when trained on varying proportions of synthetic and real data.
